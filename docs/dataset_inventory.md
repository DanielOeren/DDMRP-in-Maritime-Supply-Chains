# Dataset inventory for the DDMRP thesis

Inspected: 12 November 2026. Initial scope: **175 files** (150.8 MB); subsequently added the complete public OIL-041 sample repository (**15 files**, 9,933,448 bytes). Current scope: **190 files**, 160,719,324 bytes. Paths below are relative to `data/temp_downloads/`. Source files were preserved; no cleaning or conversion was saved.

**Recommendation:** start with the original oil/MRO workbooks in `spdf-main`, which combine consumption with delivery times, inventory parameters, prices and equipment criticality. Use manufacturing and RAF data as external benchmarks, and synthetic data for controlled robustness experiments. **OIL-041 is the richest synthetic scenario candidate**, adding timestamped consumption, supplier variability and emergency procurement, but its tables are not a validated chronological inventory simulation. These downloads support a simulation study, but do not by themselves establish that DDMRP improves a real maritime/offshore supply chain.

## Important preparation decisions

**Oil/MRO originals deserve priority over `OIL/GOEIE`.** Delivery times span 0–680 calendar days; prices span 0–82,562.59, with 1,600 zero prices. Criticality counts are populated for 10,749 materials; missing codes mean no equipment association according to `Info`, not necessarily low criticality. Treat nominal delivery times as item-level anchors, then add explicitly assumed uncertainty scenarios.

The original oil columns run **Aug 2001 backwards to Jan 1997**; the prepared sheet retains this order under numeric period labels. Reverse before chronological training/testing. `Info` says whole-year blank consumption means no consumption, while zero totals can conceal withdrawals and returns. This rule must not be extended to arbitrary missing cells or other datasets. There are 3,672 negative consumption entries in the original halves. Preserve an audit trail and distinguish net consumption from gross demand; do not silently clip returns to zero. Exclude/flag **November 1997**, which the source explicitly calls unreliable, without closing the calendar gap.

There are also **three demand-cell discrepancies** between the original oil halves and `OIL/GOEIE`: materials 14515/14516 have numeric values where the originals are blank. Reconcile before selecting a canonical input. RAF and BRAF's original-data blocks differ in one price: worksheet row 3342 changes GBP 0 to 1. These are overlapping versions, not exact duplicates.

For prepared SPDF sheets, exclude item labels and price vectors from demand: `MAN/GOEIE` has 150 demand rows plus a price row after its header; SIM sheets have 60 plus a price row. BRAF/AUTO/OIL place items in rows and append a price column. Preserve original IDs when transposing or filtering.

**Synthetic aviation checks:** no missing values or duplicate part-location-month keys; issues never exceed demand; closing stock equals opening stock minus issues; stockout flags match unmet demand. Calling `generate_dataset(seed=42)` in memory reproduced the CSV's dataframe exactly. The README claims exact reproduction while the generator docstring hedges; the local comparison resolves this for the inspected version, not its publication provenance.

The generator samples lead times from **15/30/45/60 days on every monthly record**, including months with no order. When ordering, it rounds these into one or two monthly steps, allows only one outstanding order, and does not carry unmet demand forward. Its `base_stock` parameter is unused; initial stock is randomized. Reuse `demand_quantity` as input and regenerate policy outcomes. Do not interpret every `lead_time_days` row as an observed shipment or reuse `issues_quantity` as unconstrained demand. Parse dates as day-month-year.

## Supporting files: useful methods, not additional evidence

All **120 `spdf-main/Predictions/*.Rda` files** were decoded successfully: 112 forecast objects for Croston, SES, SBA, TSB, Willemain, neural-network and LightGBM variants, plus eight service-level tables. `Predictions/readme.md` identifies suffix `2` as revised output. Object dimensions vary across versions (including different item subsets); align IDs/splits before comparison. Saved service levels are prior model outputs, not observed operational performance.

`R script for master thesis.R`, `R code master thesis written out` and `lopende code` provide forecasting, preprocessing and inventory-assessment references. They assume one-period lead times, clip negative demand and sometimes remove very sparse items. The inventory calculations use stock-level/period-demand ratios rather than a full event-driven replenishment simulation. Reuse ideas selectively; recompute all thesis KPIs. `README.md` and `CITATION.cff` provide repository context/citation information, not proof of each underlying dataset's provenance or reuse terms.

The aviation `generate_obj1_synthetic_demand.py`, `validate_dataset.py`, `literature_validation.py` and README support reproducibility, structural checks and ADI/CV² classification. They do not validate maritime realism. The manuscript/documents mentioned in that README are absent from this download.

Attribute the publisher and observe the noncommercial terms. Its calibration/validation claims are publisher claims, not independently verified evidence.

Read-only checks found **no empty fields, duplicate primary IDs or unresolved references** to the part/facility/warehouse/supplier/stockout masters. Warehouse–facility mappings are consistent; movement and stockout part–warehouse pairs exist in inventory. Inventory values match stock × part unit cost to USD 0.02. These checks establish structural consistency, not causal realism.

Material limitations for simulation:

- **Chronology:** 218 of 450 emergency orders precede the start of their linked stockout. Do not replay these as causal stockout responses or derive emergency-response times without resolving that inconsistency.
- **Incomplete operational linkage:** lead-time rows have neither dates nor purchase-order IDs; procurement has no receipt timestamp. Consumption has no warehouse ID or movement link; transfers lack a paired transaction ID. Inventory has no snapshot date. Stock-balance conservation and service-level denominators therefore cannot be reconstructed reliably from the supplied schema alone. Do not count consumption and warehouse issues as two independent demand streams.
- **Scope:** timestamped event tables span 2021. Inventory covers only 4,372 of 67,500 possible part–warehouse pairs and 35 of 75 facilities, while consumption covers all 75. Six facilities are offshore platforms, but only two warehouses map to offshore facilities; the offshore subset has 2,157 consumption rows, 224 inventory pairs and 32 stockout events. A missing pair is not automatically a zero-stock observation. This is not a vessel/port logistics dataset.
- **Lead-time assumptions:** actual synthetic lead times range 2.3–365 days (median 31.6); 44.35% of rows are labeled on time. Among rows with reliability above 0.92, only **61.58%** are on time, contrary to the card's claim of at least 85%. Use inspected values and sensitivity scenarios, not the advertised validation grade, as the basis for experiments.
- **Reproducibility and evaluation:** without the generator or scorecard, seeded regeneration and the claimed reference calibration cannot be audited. Recalculate ADU/intermittency from the selected training events, reconcile its scope with master `mean_monthly_demand`, and regenerate all stock/service/emergency outcomes under each tested policy. Treat supplied stockout, cost and recommendation outputs as synthetic assumptions or diagnostics, not comparative evidence.

Recommended role: build an explicitly documented synthetic event-driven MRO scenario using its item costs/criticality, consumption and supplier-delay distributions; compare traditional replenishment and DDMRP on shared demand/disruption inputs. Retain the original SPDF oil data as the primary empirical candidate. No OIL-041 filtering, corrections or preprocessing have been saved.

## What the thesis still needs

| Model input or KPI | Available basis | Still required |
|---|---|---|
| Demand/ADU and variability | Oil, RAF, manufacturing, automotive and synthetic histories | Chronological cleaning, consistent units, training-only calibration and held-out evaluation; daily demand timing is not observed in monthly/weekly aggregates. |
| Lead-time uncertainty | Item-level nominal lead times; artificial aviation draws; OIL-041 synthetic quoted/actual lead-time records | Empirical order/receipt observations or transparent scenario assumptions for variability, disruption tails and correlated delays. Cross-item variation is not within-item delivery uncertainty; OIL-041 lacks order/date linkage. |
| Inventory value/cost | Oil prices, RAF GBP prices, manufacturing EUR costs; OIL-041 synthetic USD values, production losses and freight multipliers | Zero-price treatment, valuation convention and currency/year consistency; empirical emergency/downtime cost calibration and base freight costs remain absent. |
| Service level/stockouts | Demand streams and simulated fulfillment | Define unit fill rate separately from cycle service level; model lost sales versus backorders and count stockout events/duration consistently. |
| Emergency orders/robustness | OIL-041 has synthetic emergency orders; no measured emergency-order history | Resolve synthetic chronology issues; specify expedite triggers, costs and lead times; compare repeated scenarios with shared random inputs and uncertainty intervals. |
| MRP/DDMRP structure | Item masters, demand and some existing stock rules | BOM/dependent requirements or a clearly stated independent-demand MRO formulation; network/echelons, decoupling positions, buffer factors, spike thresholds, review rules and order constraints. No complete MRP/DDMRP setup is supplied. |

Suggested sequence: prepare the original oil/MRO data first; benchmark against its documented min–max policy and specified forecast-based replenishment; add manufacturing/RAF validation; use OIL-041, SIM and aviation scenarios for controlled robustness. Keep offshore production, LEOGO and Cognite material as optional context until a defensible link to spare-parts demand exists.
